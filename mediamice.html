Where the story began

The Problem

MediaMICE publishes across a wide editorial landscape: news, magazines, conference coverage, and industry-specific reporting for the global eye care community. During the initial review of the existing website, we identified several challenges that made it difficult for users to navigate and discover that content.

The Decision

The client chose restraint.

Minimalistic aligned most closely with the goal of making MediaMICE feel like a digital magazine, while keeping readability and content discovery intact.

That direction became the foundation of the final visual language, and was refined across navigation, content sections, article layouts, and supporting components.

The Solution

Turning a website into a magazine.

The final design works on four fronts at once: how it reads, how it is structured, how it invites browsing, and how it reaches an international audience.

A Editorial Visual Language

Content first. A minimal visual language keeps articles, images, and headlines as the primary focus of the experience.

Strong hierarchy. Typography, spacing, and section dividers separate headlines, categories, and supporting content the way a print spread would.

Selective accents. MediaMICE's vibrant brand colours appear in small doses, enough for personality, never enough to compete with the story.

B Structure & Navigation

Simplified navigation. Major areas, news, subspecialties, conference coverage, magazines, were reorganised into a hierarchy users can hold in their head.

Clear categories. Content is grouped into recognisable sections, so readers identify what they want without navigating unnecessary layers.

Improved discoverability. Search and key navigation elements were given clearer placement for moving between content types.

C The Magazine Experience

An editorial homepage. Instead of a corporate landing page, the layout leads with featured stories, headlines, imagery, and industry news.

Visual storytelling. Large imagery, prominent headlines, and structured blocks give important stories real weight on the page.

Beyond articles. News, magazines, conference coverage, and specialty content sit together in one cohesive editorial platform.

D Reach & Consistency

Language switching. A dedicated selector makes the platform accessible to an international readership.

Consistent components. Navigation, buttons, cards, and categories follow one visual system across the experience.

Scalable design. The system accommodates new kinds of editorial content without breaking hierarchy or tone.

The Results

The Results

The final concept turned MediaMICE into a more cohesive, magazine-style digital experience, editorial storytelling on one side, clear discovery and navigation on the other.

Conclusion

A publisher's website should read like a publication.

The work was less about decoration than about structure: deciding what earns the centre of the page, what belongs in a category, and how a reader moves from a headline to an issue to an archive without losing their place.

The result is a platform that carries MediaMICE's range of editorial content in one voice, and reads like the magazine it always was.
